# Present Continuous

## Core concept

Use the present continuous when the focus is on something happening now, at the time of speaking.

## Mental model

Think: **in progress now**, rather than a general fact or something that happens regularly.

## Form

```text
subject + am/is/are + verb-ing
```

For questions, put `am/is/are` before the subject.

## Usage

- describe an action happening now
- ask what somebody is doing now
- make negative statements about an action that is not happening now

## Contrast

Use the present simple for things that are generally true or happen regularly. Use the present continuous when the action is happening now.

The source also notes a group of verbs normally used in the present simple rather than the present continuous at this level, including verbs such as `know`, `understand`, `want`, and `need`.

## Common mistakes

- Missing `am/is/are` before `verb-ing`.
- Using the base verb after `am/is/are` instead of `verb-ing`.
- Using present continuous for a regular/general situation where present simple is needed.

## Original examples

- I'm reviewing a pull request right now.
- She's talking to a client at the moment.
- Are they testing the service now?
- I'm not watching the deployment dashboard.

## Recognition drills

1. Which sentence describes something happening now: “I review code every day” or “I'm reviewing code now”?
2. In “Are they testing the service?”, what word order makes it a question?

## Production drills

1. Make a sentence about what you are doing right now.
2. Turn “He is reviewing the pull request” into a question.
3. Make “They are testing the service” negative.

## Speaking drills

- What are you doing right now?
- Are you using your computer now?
- What are you looking at?
